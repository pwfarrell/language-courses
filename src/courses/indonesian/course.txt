U|Sudah or belum?|Sudah atau belum?|hourglass|Verbs never change form. Small words like *sudah*, *sedang*, and *akan* carry the time.
G|Indonesian verbs have no tenses. *Saya makan* can mean I eat, I ate, or I'm eating. When timing matters, add a marker before the verb or a time word like *kemarin* or *besok*.
X|Saya sudah makan.|I've eaten. / I already ate.

X|Saya sedang makan.|I'm eating (right now).

X|Saya pernah makan durian.|I've eaten durian (at some point).
G|*Belum* (not yet) is the polite answer to *sudah* questions, even when the real answer is no: *Sudah menikah?* *Belum.* (Married? Not yet.)
G|Question words can go first or, in conversation, right where the answer would be: *Anda pulang kapan?* (You go home when?) Yes/no questions just use rising intonation, or *apakah* at the start in formal speech.
G|*Karena* (because), *tapi* (but), *kalau* (if), and *jadi* (so) join clauses just like their English equivalents.

U|Health and emergencies|Darurat|cross|Learn *Tolong!* (Help!) and the emergency number, 112.
G|*Sakit* means both sick and hurt: *Saya sakit* (I'm sick), *Kepala saya sakit* (my head hurts). Put the body part first, then *sakit*.
X|Saya sakit.|I'm sick.
X|Perut saya sakit.|My stomach hurts.
X|Saya perlu dokter.|I need a doctor.
G|Pharmacies (*apotek*) are everywhere, and pharmacists will often recommend something for common problems. For anything serious, ask for the nearest hospital: *Rumah sakit terdekat di mana?*
G|The general emergency number is 112, and the police are 110.
