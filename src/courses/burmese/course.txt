U|Greetings|နှုတ်ဆက်ခြင်း|chat|Add ပါ (ba) to sound polite. It softens almost any sentence.|hnoke-seq-chin
G|မင်္ဂလာပါ (mingalaba) works at any time of day and in any setting. It literally means "it is auspicious."
G|Burmese sentences end in small particles that set the tone. ပါ (ba) makes a sentence polite, တယ် (deh) marks a plain statement, and လား (la) turns it into a yes/no question.
X|နေကောင်းလား|Are you well?|nay-kaung-la
X|နေကောင်းပါတယ်|I'm well.|nay-kaung-ba-deh
G|To make a verb negative, wrap it: မ (ma) goes in front and ဘူး (bu) goes at the end.
X|နားလည်တယ်|I understand.|na-leh-deh
X|နားမလည်ဘူး|I don't understand.|na-ma-leh-bu

U|Letters|အက္ခရာ|text:က|Letters come in pairs: plain က and breathy ခ. The breathy one adds a puff of air.|eq-kha-ya
G|Burmese script has 33 consonants, and each one carries a built-in "a" sound. Vowel signs sit above, below, before, or after a consonant to change that vowel.
G|Many consonants come in pairs: a plain one and a breathy one with a puff of air. က (ka) and ခ (kha), တ (ta) and ထ (hta), ပ (pa) and ဖ (hpa). They sound alike to English ears at first, so listen for the breath.
G|အ is a silent carrier for a vowel at the start of a word, as in အမေ (a-may, mother).
G|Words run together with no spaces. A space usually marks the end of a phrase, much like a comma.
L|k, g, ng, s|script
w|က|ka|Plain k with no puff of air, like the k in “skin.”||ka
w|ခ|kha|k with a puff of air, like the k in “kit.”||kha
w|ဂ|ga|g as in “go.”||ga
w|င|nga|ng as in “sing,” but it can start a word.||nga
w|စ|sa|Plain s.||sa
w|ဆ|hsa|s with a puff of air.||hsa
L|t, n, p, m|script
w|တ|ta|Plain t, like the t in “stop.”||ta
w|ထ|hta|t with a puff of air, like the t in “top.”||hta
w|န|na|n as in “no.”||na
w|ပ|pa|Plain p, like the p in “spin.”||pa
w|ဖ|hpa|p with a puff of air, like the p in “pin.”||hpa
w|မ|ma|m as in “me.” You already know it from မ … ဘူး.||ma
L|l, y, w, th, h|script
w|လ|la|l as in “love.”||la
w|ယ|ya|y as in “yes.”||ya
w|ဝ|wa|A perfect circle. It’s the shape of Wa, your guide.||wa
w|သ|tha|th as in “thin.”||tha
w|ဟ|ha|h as in “hat.”||ha
w|အ|a|A silent carrier. Words that start with a vowel are written with အ.||a

U|Food & drink|အစားအသောက်|cup|Verbs come last. Order by naming the item, then the amount, then ပေးပါ.|a-sa a-thauk
G|The verb comes last. ထမင်း စား is literally "rice eat." Objects, places, and times all come before the verb.
G|To order, name the item, then the amount with its counting word, then ပေးပါ (pay-ba, please give).
X|လက်ဖက်ရည် တစ်ခွက် ပေးပါ|One tea, please.|laq-pheq-yay ta-khweq pay-ba

G|ထမင်းစားပြီးပြီလား (have you eaten?) is a greeting more than a question. The usual reply is စားပြီးပါပြီ (sa-pyi-ba-bi, I've eaten).

U|Numbers & money|ဂဏန်းနဲ့ ငွေ|text:၁၂၃|Burmese has its own digits. You’ll see ၁ ၂ ၃ on prices and bus numbers.|ga-nan neh ngway
G|Burmese has its own digits, ၀ ၁ ၂ ၃ ၄ ၅ ၆ ၇ ၈ ၉, and they work just like 0 to 9: ၂၅ is 25. You'll see them on prices, buses, and license plates.
G|When counting things, the number comes after the noun, followed by a counting word: လက်ဖက်ရည် တစ်ခွက် is "tea, one cup."
G|Some numbers shorten inside bigger ones. တစ် (tit, one) becomes "ta" in တစ်ဆယ် (ta-seh, ten) and တစ်ထောင် (ta-htaung, one thousand).

U|About you|ကိုယ့်အကြောင်း|person|Pick “I” by who is speaking: ကျွန်တော် for men, ကျွန်မ for women.|ko-ah-kyaung
G|The word for "I" depends on who's speaking. Men say ကျွန်တော် (kya-naw) and women say ကျွန်မ (kya-ma). The polite "you" also follows the speaker: men say ခင်ဗျား (khin-bya) and women say ရှင် (shin).
G|People are addressed with a title before their name: ဦး (U) for an older man, ဒေါ် (Daw) for an older woman, ကို (Ko) for a young man, and မ (Ma) for a young woman.
G|Polite sentences from men often end with ခင်ဗျာ (khin-bya), and from women with ရှင် (shin), a bit like adding "sir" or "ma'am."
